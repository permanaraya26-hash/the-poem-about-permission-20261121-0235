## Poem choice
Title: "The Key" (working title for the submission; the poem itself carries no title line).

Draft (v1):
  Can I do it myself?
  You start to say no —
  then stop, and open your hands.
  The key is warm from your pocket.
  I turn it.
  The door was never locked,
  only waiting to be asked.

Rationale:
- The ask is the first line, literal and childlike ("Can I do it myself?") — the
  "Me Do It" / "Let Me" directness that won last time.
- Concrete image throughout: hands, a warm key, a door. Nothing abstract.
- The turn/ending lands the insight: the door was never locked — the obstacle was
  never the lock, it was the asking. Permission is the thing that was missing, and
  the asking is what supplies it. True about agency, recognisable at once, not a slogan.
- "You start to say no — then stop" is the handing-over moment: the giver's agency
  yielding to the asker's. Both sides of the exchange are in the poem.
- ~170 chars, well under 280. No rhyme forced; the em-dash and short final lines
  give the rhythm.
